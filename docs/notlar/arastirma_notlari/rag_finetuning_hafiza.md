# RAG vs. Fine-tuning vs. "Company Memory" for a Small Dealer Assistant (PortatifEv, 37-project catalog) — evidence as of 2026-09-21

Scope note: all prices below were read from the cited pages on 2026-09-21 unless another date is given. Several vendor pages are long and were read through an automated summarizer; where two reads of the same page disagreed, the disagreement is flagged rather than resolved. The context assumed throughout: a 37-project catalog (m², room program, dimensions, CAD), price lists, loading lists (BOM), past quotes and WhatsApp threads; current stack = gpt-4o-mini + JSON-schema intent extraction (~3,000 tokens/message) + deterministic rules; owner is a non-developer; hosting = Python cron on shared hosting / small VPS.

## Key Question 1 — Fine-tuning: pricing (2026), how many examples, why it is a poor way to inject facts, when it is worth it

### Takeaway
Fine-tuning has become *harder to buy* from the big API vendors in 2026 (OpenAI is winding its platform down for new customers; Google removed tuning from the Gemini API; Anthropic only ever offered it for the old Claude 3 Haiku via Bedrock), while open-weight LoRA tuning (Together, Fireworks, Vertex Gemini Flash-Lite) costs only a few dollars per run. The peer-reviewed evidence is consistent: fine-tuning is for behaviour (tone, format, classification, Turkish phrasing), not for facts like prices and catalog specs — RAG/prompting beats it for knowledge and fine-tuning on new facts measurably increases hallucination.

**Why fine-tuning is a poor way to inject facts (peer-reviewed)**
- Ovadia et al., "Fine-Tuning or Retrieval? Comparing Knowledge Injection in LLMs" (EMNLP 2024): "RAG consistently outperforms it [unsupervised fine-tuning], both for existing knowledge encountered during training and entirely new knowledge"; "LLMs struggle to learn new factual information through unsupervised fine-tuning"; exposing the model "to numerous variations of the same fact during training could alleviate this problem" — [arXiv 2312.05934](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.05934); [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.15/)
- Gekhman et al., "Does Fine-Tuning LLMs on New Knowledge Encourage Hallucinations?" (EMNLP 2024): "fine-tuning examples that introduce new knowledge are learned significantly slower than those consistent with the model's knowledge"; once learned they "linearly increase the model's tendency to hallucinate"; conclusion: "large language models mostly acquire factual knowledge through pre-training, whereas fine-tuning teaches them to use it more efficiently" — [arXiv 2405.05904](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.05904); [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.444/)

### Inferences
- For PortatifEv, fine-tuning on OpenAI is effectively off the table (no new organisations since May 7, 2026; all new jobs end Jan 6, 2027), and Gemini API tuning no longer exists; the only "one-click" managed paths left are Vertex AI (GCP project, IAM, billing — heavy for a non-developer) or LoRA on Together/Fireworks (open-weight models, cheap but requires dataset curation and a separate inference endpoint).
- Cost of a tone/format LoRA is trivial: 300 curated Turkish dealer exchanges × ~500 tokens × 3 epochs ≈ 0.45M training tokens → ≈ $0.15 at Together's $0.34/M (billed at the $4 minimum) or ≈ $0.23 at Fireworks; on Vertex ≈ $0.68 (Flash-Lite) or $2.25 (2.5 Flash). The real cost is human time to write/clean 50–500 examples and to re-run evaluation every time the base model is deprecated (OpenAI: fine-tuned models die with their base model).
- The catalog (37 rows), prices and BOMs change; every change would require a retrain and re-evaluation, and the Gekhman result implies each retrain on "new facts" raises hallucination risk. This is the strongest argument against fine-tuning for catalog knowledge in a company without ML staff.
- Where fine-tuning *is* justified later: (a) intent/route classification if a cheap model misclassifies Turkish dealer slang, (b) enforcing the dealer-style Turkish phrasing and quote format once ≥100 clean examples exist. But OpenAI's own threshold ("50–100 examples") is the same order of magnitude as a few-shot prompt, so the first experiment should be putting 10–20 of those examples in a cached system prompt.

## Key Question 2 — RAG for a 37-item structured catalog: whole-catalog-in-prompt vs vector RAG vs structured retrieval; PDF chunking; evaluation; token-cost math with caching

### Takeaway
A 37-row catalog is far below the size where vector RAG pays off: Anthropic's own guidance is to put knowledge bases under ~200k tokens straight into the (cached) prompt, and at 2026 small-model prices a ~6k-token catalog costs roughly $0.001 per WhatsApp message even without caching. Large, tabular artifacts (price lists, loading lists/BOMs, past quotes) should be converted once into structured tables and fetched *by key* (project ID, m² range, room count) rather than chunk-embedded; vector/hybrid RAG only becomes relevant for free-text material such as thousands of past WhatsApp threads.

### Cited Findings

**When RAG is unnecessary**
- Anthropic (Contextual Retrieval, Sept 2024): "If your knowledge base is smaller than 200,000 tokens (about 500 pages of material), you can just include the entire knowledge base in the prompt"; with prompt caching this cuts "latency by > 2x and costs by up to 90%" — [Anthropic contextual retrieval](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/contextual-retrieval)
- Adding more retrieved passages is not free: "the quality of generated output initially improves first, but then subsequently declines as the number of retrieved passages increases", attributed to "hard negatives" (irrelevant but similar passages) — Jin et al., "Long-Context LLMs Meet RAG" (arXiv Oct 2024, ICLR 2025) — [arXiv 2410.05983](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05983)

**If/when real RAG is needed (large free-text corpora, PDFs)**
- Anthropic's contextual retrieval results: contextual embeddings cut retrieval failure rate by 35%; contextual embeddings + contextual BM25 by 49%; adding reranking by 67% (5.7% → 1.9%); one-time cost to generate chunk contexts with prompt caching "approximately $1.02 per million document tokens"; best embeddings were Gemini and Voyage; retrieving top-20 chunks performed best vs 5 or 10 — [Anthropic contextual retrieval](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/contextual-retrieval)
- OpenAI hosted file search (Responses API): "Tool call: $2.50 / 1k calls"; "Storage: $0.10 / GB per day (1 GB free)" — [OpenAI pricing](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing). The Assistants API (the environment of the earlier 200–300k-token attempt) shuts down August 26, 2026 — [OpenAI deprecations](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/deprecations)

**Evaluation**
- RAGAS positions itself as moving "from 'vibe checks' to systematic evaluation loops"; RAG metrics: Faithfulness, Response Relevancy, Context Precision, Context Recall, Context Entities Recall, Noise Sensitivity; metrics are "LLM-driven" (LLM-as-judge) and it offers synthetic "Testset Generation for RAG"; no minimum test-set size is stated — [RAGAS docs](https://docs.ragas.io/en/stable/)

**Prompt caching mechanics and prices (needed for the cost math)**
- OpenAI: cached input billed at "0.1× the uncached input-token rate" for GPT-5.6 and later ("discounted up to 90%"); minimum cacheable prefix "1,024 tokens for GPT-5.6 and later"; earlier models report cached tokens rounded "down to the nearest multiple of 128"; earlier models' in-memory cache entries "typically remain active for around 5 to 10 minutes of inactivity, up to one hour", a `24h` retention option exists, and GPT-5.6+ support a 30-minute TTL via `prompt_cache_options.ttl`; guidance: "Put stable developer instructions and shared reference material first" — [OpenAI prompt caching guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/prompt-caching). No write premium is charged (caching is automatic) — [OpenAI pricing](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing)
- Anthropic: 5-minute cache writes cost 1.25× base input, 1-hour cache writes 2×, cache reads 0.1× (0.025× on Fable 5.1 / Mythos 5.1); minimum cacheable prompt: 512 tokens (Fable 5.1, Mythos 5.1, Opus 5, Fable 5, Mythos 5), 1,024 (Opus 4.8, Sonnet 5, Sonnet 4.6, Sonnet 4.5), 2,048 (Opus 4.7, Haiku 3.5), **4,096 (Opus 4.6, Opus 4.5, Haiku 4.5)**; "Shorter prompts cannot be cached, even if marked with cache_control"; up to 4 explicit breakpoints; cache order tools → system → messages — [Claude prompt caching docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-caching)
- Anthropic prices (per 1M): Haiku 4.5 $1 in / $5 out, 5m cache write $1.25, 1h write $2, cache read $0.10, batch $0.50/$2.50; Sonnet 4.6 $3/$15 (read $0.30); Sonnet 5 $2/$10 (read $0.20; introductory pricing through Aug 31, 2026, "scheduled increase to $3/$15 cancelled"); Opus 5 $5/$25 (read $0.50); Fable 5.1 $10/$50 (read $0.25); batch = 50% off — [Claude pricing docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)
- Google Gemini API (read 2026-09-21, extraction may be imperfect — verify): Gemini 3.8 Flash and 3.7 Flash $0.75 in / $3.75 out through Dec 31, 2026 ($1.50/$7.50 from Jan 1, 2027), context caching $0.075, cache storage $0.50 per 1M tokens per hour; Gemini 3.5 Flash $1.50/$9.00 (cache $0.15, storage $1.00/M tokens/hour); 3.5 Flash-Lite $0.30/$2.50 (cache $0.03); 3.1 Flash-Lite $0.25/$1.50 (cache $0.025); batch 50% off; free tier available on Flash models — [Gemini API pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing). Vertex figures for 2.5 Flash / Flash-Lite are in Key Question 1 — [Vertex pricing](https://cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/pricing); Vertex states context caching can "reduce the cost of Gemini input token processing by 75%".

**Turkish token overhead (affects every estimate below)**
- Measured on 2,009 professionally translated FLORES-200 sentences, Turkish costs ×1.43 as many tokens as English on GPT-4o/o200k, ×1.40 on Llama 3.1 and Gemma 2, ×1.62 on Qwen 2.5, ×2.03 on DeepSeek V3, ×2.21 on Mistral v3 (Claude and Gemini tokenizers not public) — [token-toll (GitHub)](https://github.com/mtalhasahin/token-toll)

### Inferences (token-cost math; all derived from the cited prices)
Assumptions: static prefix = system prompt + rules + full 37-row catalog table (name, m², rooms, dimensions, base price, notes) ≈ 100–150 Turkish tokens per row → ~4–6k tokens catalog + ~2k instructions ≈ **6,000 static tokens**; dynamic part (last turns + new message) ≈ 1,500 tokens; output ≈ 400 tokens (JSON + reply). Volume scenarios: 100 msgs/day (3,000/month) and 1,000 msgs/day (30,000/month).

| Model (price date 2026-09-21) | Per message, no cache | Per message, prefix cached | 3,000 msgs/mo | 30,000 msgs/mo |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| GPT-5.6 Luna ($0.10/$0.01/$0.60) | 7,500×0.10 + 400×0.60 = **$0.00099** | 6,000×0.01 + 1,500×0.10 + 400×0.60 = **$0.00045** | $2.97 / $1.35 | $29.7 / $13.5 |
| gpt-4o-mini ($0.15/$0.075/$0.60) | **$0.00137** | **$0.00092** | $4.10 / $2.75 | $41 / $27.5 |
| gpt-4.1-mini ($0.40/$0.10/$1.60) | **$0.00364** | **$0.00184** | $10.9 / $5.5 | $109 / $55 |
| Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite, Vertex ($0.10/$0.025/$0.40) | **$0.00091** | $0.00046 + storage (see note) | $2.7 + storage | $27 + storage |
| Claude Haiku 4.5 ($1/$0.10 read/$1.25 write/$5) | **$0.0095** | read case **$0.0041**; write case $0.011 | $28.5 / $12.3 (best case) | $285 / $123 |

Notes on the table:
- OpenAI caching is automatic and has no write premium, so it is "free money" once the prefix ≥1,024 tokens (GPT-5.6+) and the bot receives more than one message within the retention window (5–10 min for older models; 30-min TTL available for GPT-5.6+, 24h option on some models). A low-traffic WhatsApp bot (one message every 20 minutes) will miss most of the cache on older models but still pays nothing extra.
- Anthropic caching can *cost more* at low traffic: each 5-minute write costs 1.25× input, so if calls arrive less than once per 5 minutes every call is a write ($0.0075 for 6k tokens) and the per-message cost (~$0.011) exceeds the uncached price (~$0.0095). The 1-hour cache (2× write) breaks even at roughly 2 reads per hour (2 + 0.1n < n + 1 ⇒ n > 1.1). It pays off reliably only when one incoming message triggers several LLM calls sharing the prefix (e.g., intent extraction + answer generation + quote draft) within minutes. Also, Haiku 4.5's 4,096-token minimum means a prefix of ~3k tokens is silently not cached at all.
- Gemini explicit caching charges storage ($1 per 1M tokens per hour on Vertex/most API models): keeping a 6k-token cache alive all month ≈ 0.006 × $1 × 720 h ≈ **$4.3/month**, which exceeds the ~$1.35/month input saving at 3,000 messages. Explicit Gemini caching is therefore only worth it at high volume (unverified whether "implicit" no-fee caching applies — see Gaps).
- Bottom line: the whole-catalog-in-prompt design costs on the order of **$1–5 per month at 100 messages/day** on OpenAI's or Google's cheapest tier, and ~$30–40/month at 1,000 messages/day. A vector database, embedding pipeline and chunking code cannot pay for themselves against those numbers; they only add moving parts for a non-developer to maintain.
- Comparison with the failed agent attempt: 200–300k tokens/request × $0.15/M (gpt-4o-mini) ≈ $0.03–0.045 per request, i.e., 30–45× the cost of a 6k-token single call; on a $2/M model it would be $0.40–0.60 per request. Agent loops that re-send tool outputs and file-search results each turn are the cause; a single structured call with the catalog in the prefix avoids it.
- "Structured RAG" for this case = deterministic lookups, not embeddings: convert each price list and loading list (BOM) once from PDF into JSON/CSV/SQLite keyed by project ID (a one-time job that an LLM can do with human checking), then (1) the model chooses a project from the in-prompt catalog table, (2) plain Python fetches that project's price rows and BOM by ID and injects only those (a few hundred tokens), (3) the model formats the answer. This mirrors the Jin et al. finding that fewer, exactly relevant passages beat many similar ones, and it makes prices auditable (the number comes from a table, not from the model's memory).
- Vector/hybrid RAG (BM25 + embeddings + reranking, per Anthropic's recipe) is justified only for the unstructured corpus — past WhatsApp threads and old quotes — and even then the first version can be "search past quotes by project ID / dealer / m²" in SQLite.
- Evaluation can be lightweight: 30–50 real dealer questions with the expected project ID / price as gold answers, scored by exact match on the structured fields, plus RAGAS faithfulness on the free-text reply if desired. RAGAS gives no minimum test-set size, so this is a practitioner heuristic, not a cited number.

### Gaps

## Key Question 3 — "Company memory" / long-term memory: LangMem, mem0, Zep, OpenAI, Anthropic memory tool; what memory means in practice; what is overkill; KVKK

### Takeaway
In practice "company memory" decomposes into (1) static reference knowledge (catalog, prices, rules) → a table in the prompt or fetched by key; (2) per-dealer/customer state (who, region, past quotes, preferences) → a small database keyed by phone number; (3) conversation state → last N turns plus a rolling summary; and only (4) "learned facts extracted from chats" needs a memory product like mem0/Zep — and that layer adds an LLM call per message, a vendor, and KVKK exposure. Hosted memory vendors start free (mem0: 10k adds/1k retrievals per month; Zep: 10k credits/month) but the honest 2026 comparisons recommend picking the simplest store that fits; Anthropic's memory tool is just a client-side file directory the developer implements; OpenAI's Assistants-era memory/file-search is being retired in favour of the Responses/Conversations APIs.

### Cited Findings

**What the products actually are, and prices**

Sept 11)](https://dev.to/izgorodin/mem0-vs-zep-vs-letta-vs-cognee-vs-langmem-vs-mnemoverse-an-honest-map-of-agent-memory-in-2026-3l7b)

- Cross-cutting warning from the same comparison: "Most pain comes from picking a tool from someone else's lane because its number was bigger on a benchmark that was never about your problem." — [DEV: honest map of agent memory 2026](https://dev.to/izgorodin/mem0-vs-zep-vs-letta-vs-cognee-vs-langmem-vs-mnemoverse-an-honest-map-of-agent-memory-in-2026-3l7b)
- Anthropic memory tool: "lets Claude store and retrieve information across conversations in a directory of memory files"; "operates client-side: Claude requests file operations, and your application executes them. You control where and how the data is stored through your own infrastructure"; commands view/create/str_replace/insert/delete/rename under `/memories`; "available on all Claude 4 and later models"; enabled with `{"type": "memory_20250818", "name": "memory"}`; Python SDK ships `BetaLocalFilesystemMemoryTool`; pairs with server-side compaction; security: must block path traversal ("/memories/../../secrets.env"), strip sensitive data, cap file sizes, expire unused files — [Claude memory tool docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/tool-use/memory-tool)
- OpenAI: the Assistants API (threads + file search "memory") shuts down August 26, 2026; "The Responses API and Conversations API" are the replacement — [OpenAI deprecations](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/deprecations); file search remains a Responses-API tool at $2.50 per 1k calls and $0.10/GB/day storage (1 GB free) — [OpenAI pricing](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing)

**KVKK (Turkish data protection) — what applies when WhatsApp data goes to a foreign LLM API**
- KVKK published the "Üretken Yapay Zekâ ve Kişisel Verilerin Korunması Rehberi (15 Soruda)" on 24 November 2025 — [KVKK guide page](https://www.kvkk.gov.tr/Icerik/8547/uretken-yapay-zeka-ve-kisisel-verilerin-korunmasi-rehberi-15-soruda); [Anadolu Agency report](https://www.aa.com.tr/tr/bilim-teknoloji/kvkk-15-soruda-uretken-yapay-zeka-ve-kisisel-verilerin-korunmasi-rehberi-hazirladi/3752568)
- Law-firm summary of the guide (Erdem & Erdem): controller vs processor is decided by "işleme faaliyetinin bağlamı, kapsamı ve tarafların fiili rolü" ("the context and scope of the processing and the parties' actual roles") — foreign AI providers may be data processors depending on contract and actual control; "Organizations using foreign large language models must address transfer compliance requirements under Law No. 6698's Article 9"; principles of "hukuka uygunluk, şeffaflık, doğru ve güncel olma" (lawfulness, transparency, accuracy and currency); pseudonymization where feasible; "Model çıktılarının insan denetimiyle doğrulanmasının önem taşıdığı" ("human verification of model outputs is important") given hallucination risk; data-subject rights (deletion/access/rectification) must still be honoured — [Erdem & Erdem summary](https://www.erdem-erdem.av.tr/bilgi-bankasi/uretken-yapay-zeka-ve-kisisel-verilerin-korunmasina-iliskin-rehber-yayimlandi)
- Cross-border transfer regime (Regulation of 10 July 2024, Official Gazette 32598; standard contract templates adopted 4 June 2024): four routes — adequacy decision, appropriate safeguards (standard contract: "standart sözleşmenin KVKK'ya bildirilmesi zorunludur" — notification to the Board is mandatory, within 5 business days of signature), binding corporate rules, or exceptional/"arızi, sınırlı durumlar" (occasional, limited cases, typically explicit consent). Using foreign-hosted SaaS counts: "Sunucusu yurt dışında olan bir e-posta... kullanmak... yurt dışına kişisel veri aktarımı anlamına" gelir ("using an e-mail [or CRM/AI API] service whose server is abroad means transferring personal data abroad") — [Birasyo: KVKK cross-border transfer explainer](https://birasyo.com/tr/blog/kvkk-yurt-disi-veri-aktarimi-yeni-yonetmelik-standart-sozlesme/) (secondary source; primary = [KVKK cross-border transfer guide](https://www.kvkk.gov.tr/Icerik/8143/Kisisel-Verilerin-Yurt-Disina-Aktarilmasi-Rehberi), not fetchable in this session)
- OpenAI prices "Data residency" at a "10% uplift for eligible models released March 5, 2026 or later" — [OpenAI pricing](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing)

### Inferences
- For PortatifEv the pragmatic "memory" architecture is three tables, not a memory vendor: `dealers` (phone → name, city, tier, language quirks), `interactions` (per-thread rolling summary written by the LLM after each exchange, ≤300 tokens), `quotes` (project ID, m², options, price, date). Each incoming message loads: cached static prefix (rules + catalog) + dealer row + last summary + last ~10 turns. This is what mem0/Zep automate, but at 100 messages/day the free tiers (mem0 10k adds/1k retrievals; Zep 10k credits) would already be tight on retrievals (1,000/month for mem0 Hobby vs ~3,000 messages), pushing to $19–$125/month plus another external processor under KVKK.
- Fact extraction "memory" (mem0-style) is overkill until the company has evidence that the bot forgets things that matter; the Anthropic memory tool is attractive only if the bot runs on Claude and the owner accepts implementing/securing the file store themselves (path traversal etc.) — again more code than a table.
- KVKK: WhatsApp threads contain end-customer names, phone numbers, addresses and sometimes ID/payment details. Sending them raw to a US-hosted API is a cross-border transfer requiring a legal route (standard contract + Board notification within 5 business days, or explicit consent per transfer). Practical mitigations: pseudonymize before the API call (replace phone numbers/names/addresses with tokens in Python; the model never needs them to recommend a house), keep raw threads and the dealer DB in Türkiye (VPS in Türkiye or on-prem), sign the provider's DPA, prefer EU data-residency endpoints where offered (OpenAI +10%), and keep a human in the loop on quotes — which the KVKK guide explicitly expects.
- Storing "learned facts" about identifiable people in a third-party memory cloud (mem0/Zep) multiplies the KVKK surface (another processor, another transfer). A self-hosted SQLite/Postgres on a Turkish VPS avoids that.

### Gaps
- The primary KVKK guide PDF and the KVKK cross-border guide could not be fetched (robots/timeouts); details rely on law-firm and news summaries.
- No source found on whether Anthropic or Google offer a Türkiye/EU data-residency option comparable to OpenAI's; not verified in this session.
- Mem0/Zep self-hosted resource requirements (RAM, vector DB) on a small VPS were not researched.

## Key Question 4 — LangChain/LangGraph in 2026: right tool for a small team, or plain Python + provider SDK?

### Takeaway
LangChain/LangGraph reached 1.0 on October 22, 2025 with a "no breaking changes until 2.0" promise, which removes the old churn complaint, but Anthropic's engineering guidance and 2026 practitioner write-ups still recommend the simplest possible setup — direct provider SDK calls with structured outputs — for a single-provider, few-tools, single-loop system like a WhatsApp dealer bot; LangGraph earns its keep only for multi-step workflows mixing deterministic and agentic steps with human-in-the-loop, and LangSmith observability starts free (5k traces/month) but scales to $39/seat plus usage.

### Cited Findings
- LangChain 1.0 and LangGraph 1.0 released October 22, 2025: "These 1.0 releases mark our commitment to stability for our open source libraries and no breaking changes until 2.0." New `create_agent` abstraction, middleware hooks ("allow you to customize behavior in the agent loop"), standard content blocks; "Legacy functionality moves to `langchain-classic` for backwards compatibility." Positioning: LangChain for "Agents that fit the default loop (model → tools → response)"; LangGraph for "Workflows with a mixture of deterministic and agentic components", long-running automation and "Sensitive workflows which necessitate more oversight / human in the loop" — [LangChain blog: 1.0 release](https://www.langchain.com/blog/langchain-langgraph-1dot0)
- Anthropic, "Building effective agents" (Dec 19, 2024): "we recommend finding the simplest solution possible, and only increasing complexity when needed"; frameworks "often create extra layers of abstraction that can obscure the underlying prompts and responses, making them harder to debug" and "can also make it tempting to add complexity when a simpler setup would suffice"; "many patterns can be implemented in a few lines of code"; if using a framework, "ensure you understand the underlying code." Defines workflows ("LLMs and tools are orchestrated through predefined code paths") vs agents, and the routing and prompt-chaining patterns — [Anthropic: Building effective agents](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents)
- Practitioner view (May 7, 2026): plain Python is right when the agent is "straightforward (single provider, few tools, single loop)"; "When you write the sixty lines yourself, debugging is reading sixty lines instead of navigating five layers of class hierarchy"; "the frameworks that wrap a five-line pattern in three layers of class hierarchy will cost you when something breaks"; names Pydantic AI and provider-SDK structured outputs as thin alternatives — [tinyagents.dev: LangChain alternatives 2026](https://tinyagents.dev/blog/langchain-alternatives)
- LangSmith official pricing: Developer "$0 / seat per month", "1 seat", "Up to 5k base traces / mo, then pay-as-you-go"; Plus "$39 / seat per month", "Up to 10k base traces / mo"; pay-as-you-go "$1.50 / LCU" compute and "$1.00 / LSU" storage; base traces retained 14 days, extended 400 days; Plus includes "1 free small serverless deployment" — [LangChain pricing](https://www.langchain.com/pricing)
- A third-party guide (June 11, 2026) claims per-trace overage is now "$2.50 per 1k traces" (base) and "$5.00 per 1k traces" (extended) and that older "$0.50 per 1k" figures are outdated; the official page fetched here showed LCU/LSU units rather than per-1k prices, so this is unverified — [Inference.net LangSmith pricing explainer](https://inference.net/content/langsmith-pricing/)
- Memory-tool comparison notes LangMem is only the "Honest default" for teams already on LangGraph — [DEV: honest map of agent memory 2026](https://dev.to/izgorodin/mem0-vs-zep-vs-letta-vs-cognee-vs-langmem-vs-mnemoverse-an-honest-map-of-agent-memory-in-2026-3l7b)

### Inferences
- The existing design (one structured-output call → deterministic rule layer) is exactly the "workflow" pattern Anthropic describes; it is already framework-free and should stay that way. Adding LangChain would wrap the same single call in abstractions and add a dependency tree that a non-developer cannot debug on shared hosting.
- LangGraph becomes worth considering only if the bot grows into a multi-step quote pipeline with checkpoints (e.g., "collect requirements → choose project → compute price → human approval → send PDF") and the team wants persisted state/retries out of the box. Even then, a plain state machine in Python with a SQLite table of "open quote sessions" is within reach and avoids LangGraph Platform/LangSmith costs.
- Observability need not be LangSmith: logging every request/response JSON and token usage to SQLite or a Google Sheet provides the audit trail the owner needs and keeps data in Türkiye.

### Gaps
- No quantitative 2026 survey of framework adoption by small teams was found; the recommendation rests on Anthropic's guidance and one practitioner article.
- LangGraph Platform (deployment) per-node pricing was not confirmed from the official page in this session.

## Key Question 5 — Turkish language quality of small/cheap models (benchmarks 2024–2026)

### Takeaway
Benchmarks agree that (a) model size matters a lot for Turkish — sub-10B open models score 30–46% on TurkBench while 27B+ models reach 73–79% — (b) Turkish-specific fine-tunes of small models generally *underperform* strong multilingual models of similar size, and (c) prompting in Turkish beats prompting in English. Among proprietary models the only published Turkish scores are older (GPT-4o 83.1%, Claude 3 Opus 79.1% on TurkishMMLU, 2024); no 2026 benchmark score was found for gpt-4o-mini, GPT-5.6 Luna, Claude Haiku 4.5 or Gemini Flash specifically. Llama 4 does not list Turkish as a supported language; Qwen3 and Gemma explicitly do. Turkish costs ~1.4× the tokens of English on OpenAI/Llama/Gemma tokenizers, which should be built into every cost estimate.

### Cited Findings
- TurkishMMLU (2024; 10,032 questions from the Turkish education system; >40 models): 5-shot accuracy on TurkishMMLU_sub — GPT-4o 83.1%, Claude-3 Opus 79.1%, GPT-4-Turbo 75.7%, Llama-3 70B-IT 67.3%, Claude-3 Sonnet 67.3%; Turkish-adapted Trendyol-LLM 7B 34.1%; "mathematics is the most difficult subject for almost all models"; Turkish-adapted models "are significantly behind newer variants of similar size (Llama-3 8B)"; CoT helped mathematics (+25.0% for GPT-4o) but not Turkish Language & Literature — [TurkishMMLU (arXiv 2407.12402)](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.12402v2)
- TurkBench (Jan 11, 2026; 8,151 samples, 21 tasks, 27 open-weight models; gpt-4o-mini used as the judge for open-ended tasks): averages — gpt-oss-120b 78.6%, GLM-4.6 76.9%, DeepSeek-V3.1 75.2%, Qwen3 80B/30B 75.0–73.4%, Gemma-3-27B 73.0%, Qwen2.5-14B 66.5%; small models: Llama-3.1-8B 45.7%, Phi-4-mini 42.1%, Gemma-2-2b 38.6%, Qwen3-1.7B 36.5%, Qwen3-0.6B 29.5%; Turkish-specific gemma-3-12b-TR-V1 71.2% and TR-Gemma-9b 65.3% "underperforming larger multilingual counterparts"; "Turkish-language prompts consistently outperform their English equivalents"; "even state-of-the-art systems struggle with culturally grounded reasoning" — [TurkBench (arXiv 2601.07020)](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.07020v1)
- Cetvel (EACL 2026; 23 tasks in 7 categories incl. grammatical error correction, MT, culturally grounded QA; 33 open-weight LLMs up to 70B): "Turkish-centric instruction-tuned models generally underperform relative to multilingual or general-purpose models"; grammatical error correction and extractive QA best differentiate models — [Cetvel (arXiv 2508.16431)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.16431); [ACL Anthology EACL 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.eacl-long.46/); [code](https://github.com/KUIS-AI/cetvel)
- TR-MMLU (Jan 2025; 6,200 questions, 62 sections drawn from 280k questions across 67 disciplines) reports that "the impact of tokenization and fine-tuning strategies" is a significant factor in Turkish performance — [TR-MMLU (arXiv 2501.00593)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00593)
- Llama 4 model card: officially supported languages are "Arabic, English, French, German, Hindi, Indonesian, Italian, Portuguese, Spanish, Tagalog, Thai, and Vietnamese" — Turkish is not among them, although "pre-training includes 200 total languages" and developers "may fine-tune Llama 4 models for languages beyond the 12 supported languages" — [Llama 4 MODEL_CARD.md](https://github.com/meta-llama/llama-models/blob/main/models/llama4/MODEL_CARD.md)
- Qwen3 (released April 29, 2025) supports "119 languages and dialects", with Turkish explicitly listed in the Turkic family; sizes 0.6B–32B dense plus Qwen3-30B-A3B and Qwen3-235B-A22B MoE — [Qwen3 blog](https://qwenlm.github.io/blog/qwen3/)
- Token overhead: Turkish costs ×1.43 (GPT-4o/o200k), ×1.40 (Llama 3.1, Gemma 2), ×1.62 (Qwen 2.5), ×2.03 (DeepSeek V3), ×2.21 (Mistral v3) as many tokens as the same English sentences (FLORES-200, 2,009 sentences) — [token-toll](https://github.com/mtalhasahin/token-toll)
- A community Turkish MMLU leaderboard exists (Gradio app) but its table could not be read in this session — [Turkish MMLU Leaderboard (HF Space)](https://alibayram-turkish-mmlu-leaderboard.hf.space/); EleutherAI's lm-evaluation-harness ships a `turkishmmlu` task for reproducible testing — [lm-evaluation-harness turkishmmlu](https://github.com/EleutherAI/lm-evaluation-harness/blob/main/lm_eval/tasks/turkishmmlu/README.md)

### Inferences
- For a Turkish dealer bot, the safest cheap options are the small tiers of frontier vendors (GPT-5.6 Luna / gpt-4o-mini class, Gemini Flash-Lite, Claude Haiku 4.5) rather than sub-10B open models: TurkBench shows 8B-class open models around 45% average on Turkish tasks, whereas the TurkBench authors trusted gpt-4o-mini enough to use it as the Turkish judge. If an open-weight model is wanted (for LoRA style tuning or data residency), Qwen3 30B-A3B / Gemma-3-27B-class models (73–75% on TurkBench) are the evidence-backed floor; Llama 4 and Mistral are poorer choices for Turkish (unsupported language; 2.2× token cost for Mistral).
- Write the system prompt, few-shot examples and JSON field descriptions in Turkish (TurkBench: Turkish prompts outperform English ones).
- Do not expect a Turkish-specific fine-tune of a small model to fix quality; three independent benchmarks (TurkishMMLU, TurkBench, Cetvel) found such models underperform general multilingual models. Fine-tune only for style/format, on top of a strong multilingual base.
- Budget 1.4× English token counts for all Turkish text; the cost table in Key Question 2 already assumes Turkish-sized rows.

### Gaps
- No published Turkish benchmark scores were found for gpt-4o-mini, GPT-5.6 Luna/Terra, Claude Haiku 4.5, Gemini 2.5/3.x Flash or Llama 4 (TurkBench and Cetvel evaluate open-weight models only; TurkishMMLU's proprietary results are from 2024). Community anecdotes were not collected in this session; a 30-question in-house Turkish test on real dealer messages is the practical substitute.
- Claude and Gemini Turkish tokenizer overheads are unknown (tokenizers not public).
